# Bounded startup and recovery

Status: draft · 2026-10-29, partly shipped: item 3 for steers
([task steering](../specs/task-steering.spec.md) 3.3–3.4, 3.6)

## Context

Two open items already cover recovery in general:
[worker efficiency](2026-10-24.worker-efficiency.draft.md) P4 (interruptions keep
the work) and P6, which [failure classes](../specs/failure-classes.spec.md)
implemented except for the `session` class, which keeps today's retry until P4
lands. This draft adds three narrow rules from
[Cloudroom](https://github.com/davidondrej/cloudroom)
(`core/docs/session-lifecycle.md`) that neither covers. If P4 is specified
first, fold them into it.

## Proposal

1. **Throttle session starts, not running sessions.** When a node restarts with
   many claimable tasks, it starts at most half the logical CPUs' worth of session
   containers at once (minimum one); the rest queue. Starts get a longer deadline
   than ordinary operations. Cloudroom uses 120 s against 30 s. This bounds the
   thundering herd that turns one restart into a wave of `timeout`/`stalled`
   failures. It does not cap concurrency once sessions are running.
2. **One recovery per unexpected exit, no loop.** A session that exits
   unexpectedly gets exactly one automatic resume after its old container is
   confirmed gone. A later automatic attempt needs new progress in between (a
   milestone, a completed turn) or an operator action. A crash that recurs with
   no progress in between fails the attempt with its cause, rather than spending
   the remaining `max_attempts`.
3. **Uncertain work is never resent.** Work that was dispatched to a session but
   has no recorded outcome when that session dies becomes `unknown`, and is not
   replayed into the next attempt. Examples are a steering message or a delivery
   step. The next attempt is told it may have happened. Cloudroom's equivalent is
   `unknown_after_restart`.

## What Argus has today

NanoClaw's startup adoption gate (`patch-startup-adoption-gate.py`) adopts running
sessions after a restart. Retries follow failure classes and `max_attempts`.
Steering is delivered on heartbeat
([task steering](../specs/task-steering.spec.md)). A steer dispatched and never
acknowledged now ends `unknown` with its attempt, and the next attempt gets the
count, not the text (item 3 for steers). Other dispatch-then-confirm writes keep the
audit in the [research](../research/2026-10-29.bounded-recovery.research.md) §3.

## Success

- Restarting a disposable node with more queued tasks than half its CPUs creates
  no start-timeout failures, and every task starts.
- A fixture harness that crashes on start consumes one automatic recovery and
  then fails with its cause, not `max_attempts` retries.
- A steering message dispatched just before a crash is reported as `unknown` and
  is not delivered again.

## Open questions

- Does NanoClaw start session containers concurrently on restart today, and where
  would the throttle live: the channel adapter or `container-runner.ts`?
- Which existing writes are dispatch-then-confirm, and therefore need an
  `unknown` outcome? Audit together with §2 of the
  [task operations draft](2026-10-29.task-operations.draft.md).

## Out of scope

Exactly-once external side effects, and resuming native sessions (P4).
